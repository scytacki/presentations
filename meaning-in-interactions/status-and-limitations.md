# Status & Limitations

*An honest note on the state of this compendium — written both for me picking it back up later and for
a collaborator reading it for the first time. Read it before relying on any single document. As of
2026-07-08.*

This work was produced quickly, with far more research surfaced than fully digested. It is genuinely
useful as it stands, but it is a **first pass**, not a finished report. The caveats below are the
things I'd want a reader to know — and the concrete work I'd do next if I had the time.

---

### 1. Parts are not fully understood — and that's partly an audience question

There are parts of this compendium I don't fully understand yet, because I didn't have time to ask the
clarifying questions that would resolve them. Someone else might understand those parts fine — and,
equally, there are probably parts I understand that another reader won't. So "cleaning this up" isn't a
single objective task: **how much to explain, and what to explain, depends on who's reading it.** Before
investing in a polish pass, it's worth deciding who the audience is.

### 2. The research directions are uneven and need a curation pass

[research-directions.md](research-directions.md) lists directions we could pursue. They are at very
different stages of development — some are well-scoped, some are sketches, and some we have **already
started on**. The document should be reviewed and edited to make that maturity explicit, and ideally
turned into **my actual recommendations** based on what I've learned, rather than a flat list.

I'd estimate this takes **about another day to do decently, and several days to do well** — because
doing it well will surface areas whose existing research we haven't yet explored, and chasing those
down is where the time goes.

### 3. `finding-meaning-problem.md` is stale

[finding-meaning-problem.md](finding-meaning-problem.md) was written *before* the research was done and
never updated afterward. Now that I understand the space better, I'm fairly sure I'd frame the problem
differently. Treat it as the original starting point, not the current view.

### 4. There's no single coherent story yet

The compendium doesn't really tell one coherent story — it's a set of related investigations. I think a
**story could be woven through it** to connect the pieces. Alternatively, it might read better **broken
into multiple smaller collections**, each self-contained and more digestible than the current whole.
Either way, the current organization is functional, not final.

### 5. Paywalled papers were not accessed

Several cited papers are paywalled and were read by neither me nor Claude (they're flagged in
[bibliography.md](bibliography.md), including a ranked "to obtain" list). I believe there's still useful
work to be done without them. But **obtaining those papers is a concrete, well-defined task**, and once
they're in hand, an AI could be asked to review them *alongside this compendium* — to check that our
summaries are accurate, and to identify new areas that should be clarified or added.
